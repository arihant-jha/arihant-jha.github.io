1/ You are worthy, because you are a human. 

2/ Solve problems by creating new knowledge.

3/ Knowledge creation is a hard (not easy) yet the process issimple, governed by the loop: 
1. Identify an important problem (important to you)
2. Conjecture: Invent an explainatory theory to solve the problem
3. Criticize: Attempt hard to falsify the conjecture
4. Accept the conjecture as a new explainatory theory if it passes criticisms, else iterate through the next cycle

>On a side note even though you fail, you did create new knowledge; which means you have a better chance at next attempt and better chance at solving problems.

4/ Follow your curiosity, even though your brain keeps exploding with ideas — it's not enough; an idea is just the starting point, it's just a new conjecture — you need to iterate through to complete the loop and create new useful knowledge.

5/ Work Hard and become excellent! *Excellence is the quality to take the pain*
- Go to a cafe. Drink coffee. Make Great Things. Work on important problems. *Important to you!* Ship, put things out in the world. 
- Work/Talk/Listen with/to/about excellent engineers and makers of things.
- Be obsessed about solving the problem - because you are limited by time, thus make sure you're picking the most important problem and spending incredible amount of time running the loop.
- Have fun - cultivate the environment so that all types of idea creation align: explicit, implicit and unconcious.
- Run

6/ Don't conform to authority, people around you or anyone. Form your own world view and think for yourself.

7/ Disobidience is not a character flaw, it's a quality to embrace. If you're not disobidient — you'll follow the path of least resistance set by an authority figure (a human, your own mind, temptations or worse a program).

Another way to think about it is - Don't be superstitious, that's the least reistant path, if you don't have an explainatory theory - you're being superstitious, always follow your best explainatory theories; if you don't have one create it and follow the loop.

Example, this is a problem you're currently fighting with (Aug 6, 2026)

When it's time to get out of bed, your brain gives you new reasons not to get up and take another nap, but if you go back and look at all the days you did that v/s you did not and check which leads to a better outcome and come up with an explainatory theory: it's a step by step process, first 30m take a lot of effort, but once you're out the door, take sunlight, go for a hard run, gain some self-respect by making the efforts, you create momentum to do good work and then you go to a cafe, work a bit more, you create more momentum

8/ Solve repeated problems once and for all. Explict theories also help you to solve the problem once and for all. You can cache the theory, and if you find a new criticism or are able to conjecture a better explainatory theory: apply the loop and update it; but you've already lived 26 years on the planet (~1/3rd of my life) and read collectively about 10,000+ human years - based on so much data, you can come up with some explainatory theories and cache them.

9/ If you have a choice to learn or solve a problem, err towards problem solving; so that when you do go and learn, you're more focused because now that problem has become a personal problem for you.

10/ When learning: Go for ideas and theories with maximum reach. Studying evolution of a field is really helpful to form a worldview on what to focus on. Don't go for popularity, always go for reach of the tools that help you do the loop: create an explainatory theory (write a program to solve a problem), conjecture it (test it out with real problems), iterate. With the goal of solving more and more ambitious problems and becoming excellent.

11/ Explore, but follow the power law or else you'll spread yourself too thin. Power law as proposed by *Paul Graham* in his essay *How to do great work*:

Attention devoted to a problem = $(m-1)/m^n$
if it's nth most important problem out of m problems in the list.

What this exposes is, I need to be really selective about what and how much I'm curious about a certain thing.

12/ Possibility matters more than Probability

Success of a startup is very low probability event, success of humans going to mars is low probability, success of me becoming a gradmaster or an olympian is low probability event.

Does that makes it less worth pursuing them? Think about the same for these questions:

Probability of ancient humans discovering fire were very low, discovering the wheel very low, the british enlightenment happening and us starting to create scientific knowledge very low, humans as a species surviving for so long very low. And now go back to the time before these discoveries and think: Was trying to pursue these goals less worth? Would it have been better those humans accepted the fate of probability? The answer is obvious.

Human lives don't run on Probability, it runs on Possibilities and because humans are falliable, it's obvious we should pursue more ambitious knowledge creation endavours; thus probability should never decide what to aim for, it should be result of the function: MAX(ambition, importance, impact on humanity, 0.1\*survival) 

*Note: Importance is importance to you, and it is correlated to what seems like an interesting possibility; you only have limited time, so pick the most important problem => the most interesting possibilty you would like to make happen)
